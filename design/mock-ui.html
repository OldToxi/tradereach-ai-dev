      Model settings

      Keys are read from server environment variables. They are never sent to the browser, never written to the repository, and rotate on a 90-day reminder.

      Refusals and limits

      AI may not fill a verified field.
      It can propose a value; a person promotes it.
      AI may not send.
      The send path requires an approval record that only a human can create.
      AI may not quote commercial terms.
      Reserved terms are stripped from the prompt context entirely.
      Low confidence surfaces.
      Below 0.6, the output is labelled uncertain and the task is routed to a person instead.
      Nothing is invented.
      A claim without a source is dropped from the draft and listed as a gap.

      Safety rails on the mail connector

      Draft-only scope.
      The OAuth grant does not include send. Even a bug cannot mail a real buyer.
      Test-domain allowlist.
      Recipients must end in .test. Anything else is refused and logged.
      Per-user tokens.
      Drafts appear in the approver's own mailbox, so ownership is obvious.
      Retry with backoff.
      Connector failures queue and surface as a visible error, never a silent drop.
      Unsubscribe is permanent.
      A suppressed address cannot be re-added by import or by AI.

      Reserved commercial matters

      Blocks outreach

      A draft containing any of these is held until a Commercial Authority releases it. Detection runs on meaning, not keywords, so "what would a container land at" is caught as pricing.

      Standard refusal language

      Used whenever a buyer asks for something reserved, so the answer is consistent and nobody improvises.

      Thank you for asking. Commercial terms for this product are set by our export desk rather than by me, so I have passed your question to Mahbub Rahman, who looks after pricing and terms for Türkiye. He will write to you directly this week. In the meantime, may I send the technical specification for the 8 lb count so your team can check it against your machine settings?

      Build notes

      The engineering view behind this mock: stack, data model, and where each requirement in the brief lives in the product.

      Proposed stack

      Backend
      Next.js route handlers + a small job runner for research and cadence
      Database

      Auth
      Auth.js with Google Workspace SSO; role claims in the session
      AI
      Anthropic API, server-side only; structured JSON outputs, schema-validated
      Connectors
      Gmail + Calendar over OAuth, compose scope only
      Queue
      Postgres-backed jobs — research runs, follow-up scheduling, reply polling
      Secrets
      Environment variables, .env.example committed, real keys never
      Tests
      Vitest on scoring and guardrails; Playwright on the ten-step demo journey

      Core tables

      product · market · company · company_field (value + provenance + source_id + confirmed_by) · source · contact · qualification · score_run · ai_run (model, prompt version, tokens, cost) · message (ai_version, human_version, approved_by, approved_hash) · thread · reply (category, confidence) · task · meeting · audit_event · user · suppression

      Provenance is a column on every fact, not a separate table — so no field can exist without one.

      Known limits of this mock

      Data is fixed and in-memory; filters demonstrate behaviour rather than query a database.
      Gmail, registry and trade lookups are represented, not live.
      Reply classification is shown with pre-computed results; the model call happens server-side in the real build.
      Scoring weights display correctly but do not recompute the sample rows.
